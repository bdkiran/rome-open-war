# Decision: supply and attrition for field armies

- **Status:** Rejected by the player. Not to be built.
- **Decides:** whether field armies lose soldiers when they are away from friendly land.
- **Why it's recorded:** so the idea isn't proposed again by accident.

## The idea

- An army in its own or a friendly region would be supplied.
- In enemy land, desert or mountains it would lose a few percent of its soldiers each turn.
- Farms would make nearby land better for foraging, and ports could supply coastal armies.
- The aim was to make *where* an army marches matter, not just how fast. Roads would get
  a strategic role, deep raids would get riskier, and big empires would be slowed down.

## Decision

The player doesn't like supply and attrition, and it won't be built.

## What covers its aims instead

| Aim | Covered by |
|---|---|
| Slow down big empires | [Public order](public-order.md): conquered cities need garrisons and cost something to hold |
| Make positioning matter | [Battles](battles.md) (frontage, digging in), [Region specialties](region-specialties.md) |
| Attrition where it already exists | Sieges only: a besieged garrison loses 5% a turn (README, Sieges). That stays. |
